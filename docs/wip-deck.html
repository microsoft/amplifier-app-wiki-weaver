Compiling a wiki instead of retrieving from documents

Nine variations measured. Two worked. Then it stopped generalizing.

The premise

Don't re-derive the knowledge on every query. Compile it once.

Karpathy's llm-wiki gist: have the LLM build a persistent, interlinked wiki from a corpus — and keep it current as the corpus grows.

We built it. Then we measured whether it beats just reading the sources.

Win one — preserve disagreement
+1.20

rubric, on its own — the single biggest quality gain in the project

Summarizers smooth. "The team converged on X" erases who objected and why. Tell the writer to record who held which position — and whether it resolved, was dropped, or was left open.

Win two — follow the citations
+1.70

rubric vs raw sources

The wiki is not a replacement for the sources. It's an index into them. Every page cites where it came from — let the answering agent open them.

9 of 10 questions gained a fact the wiki had compressed away.

The real lesson

The wiki's value scales inversely with how searchable the corpus already is.

Raw transcripts
1.90
Nearly unusable. Compilation did real work.
Raw articles
3.90
Grep already wins. Nothing left to add.
It was never "compilation beats retrieval." It was compilation beats retrieval when retrieval is hard.
The compounding bug

A fixed retrieval cap meant the writer could read six pages. Ever.

Where we are

Working and measured — for one shape of corpus.

  • Works: large unstructured conversational corpora, where raw search is hard.
  • Open: nothing forms a whole-corpus view. One theme, ten unconnected pages.
  • Caveat: n=10 questions per corpus. Directional, not tight.
